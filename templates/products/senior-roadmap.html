Florida Senior Year Roadmap · Class of 2027

Senior year at a glance

Five things decide how smoothly senior year goes. Everything else in this roadmap supports these.

1. Know every deadline

Early deadlines at Florida universities start October 15. Write each school's dates in the tracker (page 8) the first week of school.

2. File two aid forms

The FAFSA (federal aid) and the FFAA (Florida's form for Bright Futures and state aid). They are separate. You need both.

3. Lock in Bright Futures

GPA, test score, service or work hours, then the FFAA. Ask the counselor for a Bright Futures evaluation in the fall.

4. Ask early, follow up

Check which schools want recommendation letters (UF and UCF don't use them). If needed, ask teachers 3–4 weeks ahead. Check every portal weekly.

5. Compare real costs before May 1

Acceptance letters arrive first, then aid offers. Compare what you'd actually pay at each school (page 10) before paying a deposit. UF, FSU, FIU and FAU list May 1 as the deposit deadline for Early Action and regular admits. Early Decision deposits are due much sooner.

✓ This month's quick wins

  • Make a list of every school your student is applying to, with the plan for each (Early Decision, Early Action or regular)
  • Create StudentAid.gov accounts for your student and each parent who must be a FAFSA contributor (usually one)
  • Find the school counselor’s email and ask for a Bright Futures evaluation
  • Put one 20-minute “college check-in” on the family calendar every week this fall
How to use this roadmap: Read it once now. Then each month, open the month-by-month checklist (pages 4–5), check things off, and update the trackers. Use pencil, because dates and plans change.

Bright Futures: the senior checklist

Bright Futures is Florida's biggest scholarship. Requirements for the Class of 2027:

Florida Academic ScholarsFlorida Medallion Scholars
Pays100% of tuition and applicable fees at Florida public colleges and universities75% of tuition and applicable fees at Florida public universities (100% for an associate degree at a Florida College System school)
Bright Futures GPA3.50 weighted3.00 weighted
Test score (one of)SAT 1330 · ACT 29 · CLT 95SAT 1190 · ACT 24 · CLT 82
Hours100 volunteer hours, 100 paid work hours, or a combination totaling 10075 volunteer hours, 100 paid work hours, or a combination totaling 100

The Bright Futures GPA uses 16 core credits: 4 English, 4 math (Algebra 1 or higher), 3 natural science, 3 social science and 2 credits of the same world language. Honors, AP, IB, AICE and academic dual enrollment courses earn extra weight.

1 This fall

  • Ask the counselor for a Bright Futures evaluation: where does your student stand on GPA, test score and hours?
  • If a score is short, register for the next SAT, ACT or CLT. Students can test as many times as they want
  • Count documented hours. Each one must be signed by your student, a parent and the organization, plus any reflection your district requires

2 Apply: the FFAA

  • Submit the Florida Financial Aid Application (FFAA). It opens Oct 1 of senior year
  • Final deadline: Aug 31, 2027, with no exceptions. File it in the fall and save the confirmation

3 Before graduation

  • Turn in all hours by your district’s deadline
  • Finish the 16 core credits with the GPA you need (tests taken by Aug 31, 2027 still count)
Not automatic: Even a student who meets every requirement gets nothing without the FFAA. Mid-year graduates have different deadlines; ask the counselor.

FAFSA + FFAA, step by step

Two forms, two purposes. Do both in the fall of senior year.

FAFSA (federal)

Opens the door to Pell Grants, work-study, federal loans and many colleges' own aid, plus Florida need-based grants. The 2027–28 FAFSA opened Sept 23, 2026 and uses 2025 tax information.

FFAA (Florida)

Florida's application for Bright Futures and other state scholarships and grants. Opens Oct 1 of senior year. Final deadline Aug 31 after graduation.

1 FAFSA checklist

  • Your student and each parent contributor create a StudentAid.gov account (usually one parent; both if married parents filed taxes separately)
  • Gather your 2025 federal tax return and any untaxed income records
  • Your student starts the FAFSA and invites the parent as a “contributor”
  • Every contributor gives consent to share tax information. Without it, no federal aid
  • List every college your student is applying to
  • Submit, then save the confirmation and review the FAFSA Submission Summary
  • Check each college’s financial aid priority date. Many are earlier than you’d expect

2 FFAA checklist

  • Your student completes the FFAA on Florida’s student financial aid website
  • Use your student’s legal name and Social Security number exactly as the school has them
  • Submit and save the confirmation
  • If your student graduates mid-year, the deadline is Dec 31

3 Also check

  • Does any school on the list require the CSS Profile? (Mostly private colleges.)
  • Does any school require a separate scholarship application?
  • Are there county or local scholarships through your high school?
Sources: studentaid.gov (2027–28 FAFSA), FloridaBrightFutures.gov (FFAA).College Plan · 7
