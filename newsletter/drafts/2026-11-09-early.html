1. When your 9th grader wants to quit

It happens to almost everyone in the first year of high school: the club, sport or instrument suddenly feels like too much. Before you say yes or no, ask one question: is it the activity, the people or the schedule? Each has a different fix. And if it really isn't their thing, walking away to try something else is how teens find what they love. Colleges and scholarships notice depth in a few things more than a long list.

2. Bright Futures only works in Florida

Bright Futures pays at eligible Florida public or independent (private) colleges and universities. It doesn't follow a student out of state. That doesn't mean a dream school elsewhere is off the table, but it's worth knowing before a college list starts forming. Students apply on the Florida Financial Aid Application (FFAA) during senior year, between Oct 1 and Aug 31 after graduation. Source: Bright Futures Student Handbook, ch. 1.

3. Next year's classes get picked early

Course requests for next year usually happen months before this year ends. Ask the school when they're due, then ask your student: "Which class do you actually like?" That answer is often the best place to try honors, AP, AICE, IB or dual enrollment. Planning to apply to Florida's public universities? They look for two credits of the same world language, so it helps to keep that sequence going.

A Thanksgiving question

At the table, have your teen ask a grandparent: "What did you want to be when you were 15?" Then: "What job did you actually love?" It's the easiest career conversation of the year, and your teen hears that plans change and things turn out fine.

Term of the week: core credits

Bright Futures calculates its GPA from 16 core credits: English, math, science, social studies and world language, starting in 9th grade. Honors, AP, IB, AICE and dual enrollment courses add 0.5 per course. That's why 9th grade core classes matter more than many parents expect. Source: Bright Futures Student Handbook.
