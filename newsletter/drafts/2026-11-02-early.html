1. Three people who should know your 9th grader by spring

Their school counselor, one teacher they like, and a club sponsor or coach. These are the people who answer questions, open doors and, a few years from now, write recommendations. A quick hello at an open house or one email is a great start.

2. If the first report card has a C

High school classes move faster than middle school, and a lot of good students hit a bump in their first semester. What matters is what happens next: your student emails the teacher, goes to extra help, and changes one study habit. Grades in core classes do count for Bright Futures from 9th grade on, so it's worth fixing early, but one grade is not the whole story.

3. Four ways to earn college credit in high school

AP (single College Board classes), IB (a full program, with the diploma in 11th and 12th grade), AICE (Cambridge courses, from a few classes up to the AICE Diploma) and dual enrollment (real college classes, tuition-free with free books for public school students). All four add 0.5 to the Bright Futures GPA, and UF and UCF add a full point in their own GPA. Pick what fits your student, and ask the counselor before course registration.

Money fact: the exams are free

Florida law exempts students from paying fees for AP, IB and AICE exams, and College Board says Florida covers AP exam fees for all public school students. A score of 3 or higher on an AP exam earns college credit at Florida public colleges and universities. Sources: Florida Statutes s. 1007.27; College Board.

Term of the week: CAPE industry certification

Florida's CAPE (Career and Professional Education) courses let high school students earn real industry certifications in areas like health, IT and the trades. For a student who says "I have no idea what I want to do," a CAPE class is a low-risk way to try something.
