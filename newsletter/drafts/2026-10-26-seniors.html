1. The Nov 1–2 Early Action wave

If your senior is applying early to any of these, this is the week:

  • Sunday, Nov 1: UF Early Action, FAMU Early Action, FGCU Early Action, New College of Florida Early Action. Private schools too: University of Miami Early Decision I and Early Action, and Stetson Early Decision.

  • Monday, Nov 2: USF Early Action and FIU Early Action.

Nov 1 is a Sunday, so don't plan on a Monday morning fix. And submitting isn't the last step: UF Early Action applicants also complete STARS (UF's self-reported grades) by Nov 8, and several schools have a separate "materials" date later in November (UCF Nov 15, USF Nov 16, for example). After your senior submits, have them log in to each portal and look for a missing-items list. Every date is in our free Florida deadline calendar.

2. The form that decides Bright Futures

The FAFSA does not apply for Bright Futures. The Florida Financial Aid Application (FFAA) does. It's free, the student files it at floridastudentfinancialaidsg.org, and the 2027–28 version opened Oct 1. For Bright Futures it must be submitted no later than Aug 31 after graduation. Students who meet every requirement but never file it don't get the award, so file it this fall while you're in form mode.

For the FAFSA itself: the 2027–28 form uses 2025 tax information, and the student and each parent who provides information need their own StudentAid.gov account. Our guide FAFSA vs. FFAA walks through both.

3. Scholarship of the week: Equitable Excellence

Up to 100 seniors win $5,000 a year, renewable for up to three more years (up to $20,000 total). Who can apply: seniors at a public high school (private and home school students aren't eligible) with at least a 2.5 GPA, heading to an accredited two- or four-year college full-time in 2027–28. Scholarship America looks at financial need, grades, leadership and involvement, work experience and a personal statement about paying it forward. The deadline is Dec 22 at 3pm Central (4pm Eastern), so it closes in the afternoon. Details at scholarshipamerica.org.

Term of the week: FAFSA "contributor"

A contributor is anyone the FAFSA asks to provide information: the student, a parent, or a parent's spouse. Each contributor needs their own StudentAid.gov account and gives consent to pull tax information. Setting up those logins early is the easiest way to avoid a stalled FAFSA.
