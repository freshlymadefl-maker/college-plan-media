1. Bright Futures keeps raising the bar

For the Class of 2028, Florida Academic Scholars (100% of tuition at Florida public colleges and universities) now needs an SAT of 1350 (up from 1330), and Florida Medallion Scholars (75%) needs 1200 (up from 1190). GPA and hour requirements didn't change. The requirements for the Classes of 2029–2031 haven't been published yet, so plan for at least these numbers.

Curious where your student stands? Try our free Bright Futures checker.

2. Start a service hour log now

Bright Futures service hours are earned during high school, and each school district approves which activities count. (8th grade parents: ask your district whether the summer before 9th grade counts.) A simple log saves a lot of stress later: date, organization, what your student did, hours, and the supervisor's name and contact. Turn hours in at the end of every school year instead of chasing signatures senior year.

3. Dual enrollment: free college classes in high school

Florida public school students can take college classes through dual enrollment with no tuition, and the classes count for high school and college credit. Each partner college sets its own requirements. Hillsborough College, for example, asks for a 3.0 unweighted GPA plus a qualifying test score. 9th and 10th grade grades are what make a student eligible, so they matter more than many families realize.

Scholarship of the week: Bright Futures itself

Florida's biggest scholarship is worth planning for early: up to 100% of tuition and applicable fees at Florida public colleges and universities. It takes a Bright Futures GPA (3.5 for Academic, 3.0 for Medallion), one qualifying test score, and service or work hours. Our Bright Futures guide has every number in one place.

Term of the week: Weighted vs. unweighted GPA

An unweighted GPA treats every class the same (an A is a 4.0). A weighted GPA gives extra points for harder classes like Honors, AP, IB, AICE and dual enrollment. Colleges, scholarships and Bright Futures each look at GPA a little differently, so it helps to know both numbers.
