Junior year is when testing gets real, and the most common question we hear this month is "which test should my student take?" The good news: in Florida, you have three options and you only need one.

This week at a glance

  • Know: Florida accepts the SAT, ACT and CLT

  • Write down: the Class of 2028 Bright Futures scores

  • If testing this winter: ACT late registration for Dec 12 ends Sun, Nov 29

1. SAT, ACT or CLT? All three count

Florida's public universities accept the SAT, the ACT or the CLT, and so does Bright Futures. Your student needs one qualifying score, not all three. FIU, for example, says "an SAT, ACT or CLT score is required" for first-year admission, and FSU and UCF list all three as well.

A simple plan: take one full practice test of each over winter break, see which format feels better, and register for that one in the spring. The next ACT after Dec 12 is Feb 27, 2027 (register by Jan 22; late registration until Feb 9).

Sources: admissions.fiu.edu, admissions.fsu.edu, act.org.

2. Your Bright Futures targets (Class of 2028)

The test scores went up for this year's juniors:

  • Academic Scholars: 3.5 weighted GPA, plus SAT 1350 or ACT 29 or CLT 96, plus 100 service hours, 100 paid work hours, or a 100-hour mix

  • Medallion Scholars: 3.0 weighted GPA, plus SAT 1200 or ACT 25 or CLT 83, plus 75 service hours, 100 paid work hours, or a 100-hour mix

Bright Futures combines your student's best section scores across test dates, so a second test date can help. Our Bright Futures requirements guide has the details, and the free Bright Futures checker shows where your student stands in 30 seconds.

3. One thing to do over break

Start a running activities list: every club, job, family responsibility and volunteer shift, with hours per week and one line about what they did. Senior year, the Common App has room for up to 10 activities, and the same list helps track Bright Futures hours (your school district approves which activities count and how they're logged).

Scholarship to know now: the Florida Student Assistance Grant

Florida's need-based grant for public universities and state colleges starts with the FAFSA, not a separate application. UF lists awards of $200 to $2,610 a year. It's a senior-year step, but it's good to know your family will want to file the FAFSA as soon as it opens next fall. Sources: s. 1009.50, F.S.; sfa.ufl.edu.

Term of the week: superscore

A superscore combines a student's highest section scores from different test dates into one best score. Bright Futures does this, and many Florida universities do too (FSU and USF, for example), but each school sets its own policy, so check before test day.
