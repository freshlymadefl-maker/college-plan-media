1. Start a brag folder this week

Senior year comes with a lot of "list every award, activity and job since 9th grade" questions. A simple folder (paper or a phone album) makes it painless:

  • Certificates and awards, even small ones

  • Service hour logs with a supervisor's name

  • A running list of clubs, jobs, roles and dates

Five minutes now saves a stressful weekend in senior fall.

2. The PSAT in 9th or 10th grade is practice

The PSAT 8/9 and PSAT 10 are there to show your student what the test feels like and where to grow. National Merit uses the PSAT/NMSQT, which most students take in the fall of 11th grade. So if this year's score is lower than hoped, that's useful information, not a verdict. Source: College Board.

3. AP, AICE, IB or dual enrollment?

For the Bright Futures GPA, all four get the same extra weight: an A in one of these year-long courses counts as a 4.5. Dual enrollment earns real college credit, and for public school students the tuition and books are free. AP, AICE and IB college credit depends on the exam score. There's no single right path, so pick the one that fits your student's strengths and schedule, and leave room for sleep and the activity they love.

Award to know: Gold Seal

Bright Futures isn't only for students headed to a four-year university. The Gold Seal Vocational and Gold Seal CAPE awards are built for career and technical paths. One change to know: students who entered 9th grade in 2024–25 or later need 75 volunteer hours for Gold Seal (it used to be 30), or 100 paid work hours, or 100 hours combined. Source: Florida Statutes s. 1009.536.

Term of the week: PSAT/NMSQT

The PSAT/NMSQT is the version of the PSAT that doubles as the National Merit Scholarship Qualifying Test. Its name is the clue: it's the one National Merit looks at, usually in junior year.
