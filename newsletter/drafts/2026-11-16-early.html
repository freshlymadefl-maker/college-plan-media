This week at a glance

  • Ask for: a short meeting with your student's school counselor

  • Unlearn: "freshman year doesn't count"

  • Try: a few easy Thanksgiving dinner questions

1. You can ask to meet the school counselor

Many parents don't realize they can simply email the school counselor and ask for 15 minutes. Counselors have big caseloads, so a short, focused meeting goes a long way. Three questions worth bringing:

  • "Is my student on track for Bright Futures' 16 core credits?" Those are 4 English, 4 math (Algebra 1 and up), 3 natural science, 3 social science and 2 world language.

  • "What's the plan for world language?" Bright Futures counts two credits in the same language, in sequence, so it helps to know when your student will take them.

  • "How are service hours approved and logged here?" Bright Futures service hours are earned during high school, and each school district decides which activities count and how hours are documented. Ask before your student starts, not after.

Source: Bright Futures Student Handbook, ch. 1 (Florida Department of Education).

2. Myth: "freshman year doesn't count"

It does. The Bright Futures GPA is built from the 16 core credits on the high school transcript, so 9th grade English, math, science, social studies and world language grades are part of it from day one. Honors, AP, IB, AICE and academic dual enrollment courses get extra weight: 0.5 for a full-year course (0.25 for a semester course). Source: Bright Futures Student Handbook, ch. 1.

That doesn't mean 9th grade has to be perfect. It means a rough first quarter is a good moment to ask for help, not a reason to wait it out until 11th grade.

3. A few good Thanksgiving questions

The best college-planning conversation at this age doesn't sound like college planning at all. Try one of these at the table:

  • "What's the most interesting thing you learned this year, in or out of school?"

  • "If you could spend a whole Saturday on one thing, what would it be?"

  • "Which teacher makes class go fast?"

The answers point toward interests, and interests eventually point toward classes, activities and majors.

What can wait, and what matters now

Can wait: SAT prep, a college list, picking a major.

Matters now: grades in core classes, one activity your student actually enjoys, and at least one teacher who knows them well.

Term of the week: weighted GPA

A weighted GPA gives extra points for harder courses, so an A in an honors or AP class counts for more than 4.0. Bright Futures uses its own weighted GPA from the 16 core credits, and Florida universities recalculate GPA their own way, too. That's why the GPA on the report card may not match the one colleges and scholarships use.
