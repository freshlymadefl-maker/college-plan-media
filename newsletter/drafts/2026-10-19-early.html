1. Dual enrollment: free college classes in high school

Under Florida law, dual enrollment students don't pay registration, tuition or lab fees, and the books are free too. The credits count toward both a high school diploma and a college degree. To start:

  • College credit classes need a 3.0 unweighted GPA plus scores that show your student is ready for college-level work

  • Career dual enrollment needs a 2.0 unweighted GPA

That's why freshman grades matter: the 3.0 is unweighted, so honors and AP bumps don't count. Ask your school counselor which college partners with your district and when sign-ups open.

2. Start the service hour log now

Bright Futures service hours are earned during high school, and each school district decides which activities count. Ask your district whether the summer before 9th grade counts, and keep a simple log now: date, organization, hours, and a supervisor's name. It's much easier than rebuilding four years of hours in senior year.

3. Not every Bright Futures award needs a 1350 SAT

The Gold Seal Vocational award is built for career and technical students. It needs a 3.0 weighted GPA in core courses, 3 full career and technical (CTE) credits with a 3.5 unweighted GPA in those credits, minimum ACT, SAT or PERT scores, and service or work hours. If your student loves a CTE program, it's worth a look.

Term of the week: Unweighted GPA

An unweighted GPA counts every A as a 4.0, no matter how hard the class. A weighted GPA adds extra points for honors, AP, IB, AICE and dual enrollment classes. Florida uses both: dual enrollment eligibility looks at the unweighted GPA, while Bright Futures uses its own weighted GPA.
