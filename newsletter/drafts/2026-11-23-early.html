1. Thanksgiving break "college prep," ranked

Top tier: sleep, and family time with phones down. Next: ask "what class do you actually like?" and maybe drive past a college "just because." Nice to have: start a brag folder for awards and certificates, and log any service hours (your school district approves which activities count and how they're logged). Skip for now: SAT prep, a college list, essays.

Core grades do count from 9th grade on, because the Bright Futures GPA is built from the 16 core credits on the transcript. That's exactly why rest is part of the plan: rested kids do better in those classes. Source: Bright Futures Student Handbook, ch. 1.

2. Myth: "my kid has to take the SAT"

Not true in Florida. Florida's public universities accept the SAT, the ACT or the CLT, and Bright Futures accepts all three. Students need one qualifying score, not all three. The practice PSAT in 9th or 10th grade is just that, practice. Later on, a practice test of each format is the easiest way to see which one fits.

3. Before your student picks next year's classes

Course requests often come up early in the new year. Three questions to ask first:

  • "Which class felt too easy?" That might be the right place to try honors.

  • "Are you keeping the same world language?" Florida's public universities want two credits of the same language.

  • "What's one class you're excited about?" That answer is a clue to electives, activities and, eventually, majors.

The goal is challenge that fits, not the hardest possible schedule. Honors, AP, IB, AICE and dual enrollment courses do add weight in the Bright Futures GPA (0.5 for a year-long course). Sources: Board of Governors Reg. 6.002; Bright Futures Student Handbook, ch. 1.

Term of the week: core credits

Florida's Bright Futures GPA uses 16 core credits: 4 English, 4 math (Algebra 1 and up), 3 natural science, 3 social science and 2 world language. Electives matter for exploring interests, but these 16 are the ones the scholarship GPA counts.
