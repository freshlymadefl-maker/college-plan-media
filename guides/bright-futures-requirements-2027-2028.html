Last updated October 2026. Requirements can change, so always confirm with your school counselor and FloridaBrightFutures.gov.

Florida Bright Futures is the state's biggest college scholarship, and the requirements depend on your student's graduating class. Here's everything for the Classes of 2027 and 2028 in one place.

What Bright Futures pays

  • Florida Academic Scholars (FAS): 100% of tuition and applicable fees at Florida public colleges and universities.

  • Florida Medallion Scholars (FMS): 75% of tuition and applicable fees at Florida public colleges and universities.

There are also Gold Seal CAPE and Gold Seal Vocational scholarships for career and technical programs. Award amounts at private Florida schools are different.

Requirements by graduating class

Class of 2027 (this year's seniors)

  • Academic Scholars: 3.5 weighted GPA, SAT 1330 or ACT 29 or CLT 95, and 100 service hours, 100 work hours, or a combination

  • Medallion Scholars: 3.0 weighted GPA, SAT 1190 or ACT 24 or CLT 82, and 75 service hours, 100 work hours, or a combination totaling 100

Class of 2028 (this year's juniors): higher test scores

  • Academic Scholars: 3.5 weighted GPA, SAT 1350 or ACT 29 or CLT 96, and 100 service hours, 100 work hours, or a combination

  • Medallion Scholars: 3.0 weighted GPA, SAT 1200 or ACT 25 or CLT 83, and 75 service hours, 100 work hours, or a combination totaling 100

Your student only needs one qualifying score: SAT, ACT or CLT, not all three. Classes of 2029–2031: requirements haven't been published yet and have been rising, so plan for at least the Class of 2028 numbers.

The "Bright Futures GPA" is not the report-card GPA

Bright Futures recalculates GPA using only 16 college-prep core credits, with its own weighting:

  • 4 English

  • 4 math (Algebra 1 or higher)

  • 3 natural science

  • 3 social science

  • 2 world language (the same language)

It can be higher or lower than the GPA on your student's report card. Ask the school counselor for a Bright Futures evaluation.

Service and work hours

Hours must be earned during high school, and each school district approves which activities count. Ask your district whether hours from the summer before 9th grade count. Hours must be documented and signed by the student, a parent and the organization, and turned in to the school before graduation, so it's smart to turn them in at the end of every school year.

How to actually apply

Meeting the requirements isn't enough. Your student has to submit the Florida Financial Aid Application (FFAA) by August 31 after graduation. No FFAA, no Bright Futures. Don't wait; complete it during senior year.

Test scores from tests taken through August 31 after graduation are accepted for Bright Futures evaluation.

Quick answers

Does my student need the SAT and the ACT? No. One qualifying score on the SAT, ACT or CLT is enough.

When do service hours start counting? During high school. Your school district decides which activities count, so ask the counselor whether the summer before 9th grade counts.

Is Bright Futures automatic? No. Students must submit the FFAA by August 31 after graduation.

What about the Class of 2029 and later? The state hasn't published those requirements yet. Plan for at least the Class of 2028 numbers.
