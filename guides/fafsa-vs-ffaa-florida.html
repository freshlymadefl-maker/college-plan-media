Ask a room of Florida senior parents about financial aid and most will say "the FAFSA." It matters, but Florida students have a second form too: the Florida Financial Aid Application, or FFAA. It's the one that unlocks Bright Futures. Missing it is one of the most expensive mistakes a Florida family can make, and it's completely avoidable.

Here's what each form does, who files it, when, and how to get both done this fall.

The short version

FAFSA vs. FFAA at a glance

  • FAFSA (Free Application for Federal Student Aid): the federal form. It's used for Pell Grants, federal work-study and federal student loans, and many colleges use it for their own aid. In Florida it's also required for the need-based Florida Student Assistance Grant (FSAG).

  • FFAA (Florida Financial Aid Application): Florida's own form. It's how students apply for Bright Futures and several other state scholarships. It is not the federal application.

  • Do you need both? Most Florida seniors should file both. They're separate forms on separate websites.

The FAFSA: federal aid (and a lot of college aid)

The 2027–28 FAFSA is for students starting college in fall 2027, which means this year's seniors, the Class of 2027. The U.S. Department of Education opened it to the public on September 23, 2026, and it uses 2025 tax information. It's filed for free at StudentAid.gov.

The FAFSA is used for:

  • Federal aid: Pell Grants, federal work-study and federal student loans

  • College aid: many colleges use FAFSA information to award their own grants and scholarships

  • Florida's need-based grant (FSAG): the Florida Student Assistance Grant has no separate state application. It requires an error-free FAFSA submitted by each college's own deadline.

Deadlines: The federal deadline for the 2027–28 FAFSA is June 30, 2028, but that's not the one that matters. Colleges set their own priority dates, often much earlier, and some aid is first come, first served. Check the financial aid page of every school on your student's list and file as early as you can.

Some private colleges also ask for a second form, the CSS Profile, for their own aid. Each school's financial aid page will say if it's required.

The FFAA: Florida's form for Bright Futures

The 2027–28 FFAA opened on October 1, 2026. It's filed by the student (graduating seniors, or students who already graduated) on the Florida Office of Student Financial Assistance website, floridastudentfinancialaidsg.org. Your student creates a login and a Student Profile, then completes the application.

According to the state's 2027–28 FFAA guide, the FFAA is the application for:

  • Florida Bright Futures Scholarship Program (Academic Scholars, Medallion Scholars and Gold Seal awards)

  • Scholarships for Children and Spouses of Deceased or Disabled Veterans

  • Rosewood Family Scholarship

  • Florida Farmworker Student Scholarship

  • Randolph Bracy Ocoee Scholarship

The deadline that matters most

Bright Futures: the FFAA must be submitted no later than August 31 after high school graduation (August 31, 2027 for the Class of 2027). Students who meet every requirement but never submit the FFAA do not receive Bright Futures. The state says to complete it during senior year.

Other FFAA programs (Children and Spouses of Deceased or Disabled Veterans, Rosewood, Farmworker, Randolph Bracy Ocoee) have an earlier deadline: April 1.

Why so many families miss the FFAA

  • The names sound alike. Many parents assume the FAFSA covers everything. It doesn't cover Bright Futures.

  • Bright Futures feels automatic. Schools track the GPA, test scores and service hours, so families assume the award just shows up. It doesn't: the FFAA is how a student actually applies.

  • The deadline is far away. August 31 after graduation sounds like plenty of time, then summer happens. File it in the fall and it's done.

A fall plan for senior families

  1. Make your StudentAid.gov accounts. The student and each parent who needs to provide information on the FAFSA need their own account.

  2. Gather 2025 tax information for the FAFSA.

  3. Submit the FAFSA before your student's earliest college priority date.

  4. Have your student file the FFAA at floridastudentfinancialaidsg.org. It's a separate login and a separate form.

  5. Save confirmations for both forms, and check each college portal for requests for more information.

Quick answers

Is the FFAA the same as the FAFSA? No. The FAFSA is the federal application. The FFAA is Florida's application for Bright Futures and several other state scholarships. Most Florida seniors should file both.

When can my senior file the FFAA? The 2027–28 FFAA opened October 1, 2026. For Bright Futures it must be submitted by August 31 after graduation, but it's smart to do it during senior year.

Who fills out the FFAA, the parent or the student? The student, on the Florida Office of Student Financial Assistance website.

Does the FAFSA apply for Bright Futures? No. Bright Futures is not need-based, and students apply through the FFAA.

Which Florida aid needs the FAFSA? The need-based Florida Student Assistance Grant (FSAG) requires an error-free FAFSA by each college's deadline. There's no separate state application for it.

What tax year does the 2027–28 FAFSA use? 2025.
